## How I Approach Engineering Leadership

**1. Start with the business and operational risk.** Understand what must be protected, what must remain available, and what failure would mean.

**2. Translate risk into architecture and controls.** Identity, least privilege, segmentation, endpoint protection, logging, vulnerability management, recovery, and data protection should be designed into the environment rather than added afterward.

**3. Design for operations.** A control that cannot be monitored, maintained, patched, tested, or recovered is not a complete solution.

**4. Automate deliberately.** Automate repeatable work to improve consistency and response time while retaining human approval where context and risk require judgment.

**5. Measure outcomes.** Security engineering should improve detection, response, exposure, resilience, reliability, or operational efficiency—not simply add technology.

**6. Communicate trade-offs.** Leaders and engineers need clear choices around risk, cost, complexity, availability, and implementation effort.
